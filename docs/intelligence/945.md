# PSYGRID 945

At 09:45:00 IST each session, PSYGRID ranks the 989-stock universe and selects exactly one stock and a
direction (UP or DOWN). The decision is stored once and never changes; only its outcome is added later.

| Piece | Where |
| --- | --- |
| State matrix (one vector per stock) | `intelligence/matrix.py` |
| Selector, outcomes, storage | `intelligence/selector945.py` |
| Information set, live and historical pipeline | `intelligence/pipeline945.py` |
| Command line | `945.py` (`show`, `decide`, `replay`, `report`) |
| Live decision | `psygrid-intelligence` (`LiveRunner.run_945`), as soon as the 09:44 bar arrives |
| API | `/v2/945`, `/v2/945/decisions`, `/v2/945/decisions/{date}`, `/v2/945/report` |
| VM workflow | `intelligence-history.yml`, action `replay945` |

## Information set (zero look-ahead)

A decision for session D uses only:

- bars that closed by 09:45:00 on D (09:15 to 09:44), cut by `frame_at`;
- the decision universe: the 989 configured symbols plus any other symbol with a bar by 09:45. A symbol that first trades after 09:45 does not exist at 09:45;
- volume and volatility norms, the expected-response model and market-state history from qualified sessions strictly before D;
- training records (09:45 matrix plus realised outcomes) of sessions strictly before D. `decide` refuses anything else;
- option-chain and microstructure data recorded by 09:45:00.

Poisoned-future tests (`tests/intelligence/test_selector945.py`) take a day, record its decision, and then
inject enormous price moves, volume, an index shock, a stock that only trades later, a future option-chain
snapshot and a future training record. The decision hash, the input hash and every matrix value must stay
identical. Any change fails the build.

## Pipeline

```
989 stocks -> eligibility -> state matrix -> expected response / market state / microstructure / derivatives
           -> active model score -> 989 -> 1 -> UP/DOWN -> immutable decision -> (later) outcome
```

**Eligibility (STRICT)** requires all of:

- a price, a return since the open and session volatility;
- at least 80% of bars present;
- a last bar no more than 2 minutes old;
- a non-frozen price and a previous close;
- LTP ≥ ₹10;
- at least ₹1 crore traded by 09:45.

If nothing passes, the rules relax (`RELAXED`, then `MINIMAL`) and data quality is marked `DEGRADED`,
so exactly one stock is always chosen. With no usable prices at all the selector refuses to decide rather
than invent one.

## Models

| Version | What | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| v1 | Transparent rank fixed before looking at data. Opening-drive continuation: the volatility-scaled market-relative move since the open (robust z), plus 0.5 × positive relative-volume z, ±0.25 trend agreement and ±0.25 opening-range breakout agreement. Direction is the sign of the move. | Default |
| v2 | Pooled ridge regression of the absolute 15-minute forward return on 24 cross-sectionally standardised matrix features, trained walk-forward on up to 40 earlier sessions. Direction is the sign of the prediction; the pick is the largest \|prediction\|. | Active only when it earns it |
| v3 / v4 | Calibrated probability model, ensemble | Not built: the out-of-sample evidence has not justified them |

**Model choice per date** uses only that date's past. v2 is active only if, over at least 15 earlier
sessions where both models made out-of-sample picks:

- v2's mean net return beat v1's; and
- v2's pooled out-of-sample rank IC is positive with t > 2.

Otherwise v1 is active. The evidence is stored in each decision (`model.model_choice`).

## Score and probability

- `selection_score` is the percentile rank of the active model's strength among eligible stocks. It is **not**
  a probability.
- `probability` is the out-of-sample estimate of the chosen direction being right at 15 minutes:
  - for v2, a logistic calibration of |prediction| fitted on earlier days' out-of-sample stock-level predictions;
  - for v1, the Laplace-smoothed hit rate of v1's earlier out-of-sample picks (unconditional on today's score).
- `probability.status` is `CALIBRATED_OOS` only with enough evidence:
  - v2: at least 20 OOS days and an expected calibration error ≤ 0.05;
  - v1: at least 50 OOS picks.

  Otherwise it is `UNCALIBRATED`. The Wilson 95% interval of the OOS pick hit rate is always shown.

## Outcomes

- **Entry**: the open of the 09:45 bar, the first price after the decision (within 5 minutes if that bar is missing).
- **Exit**: the close of the bar ending at 09:45 + h, for h = 5, 15 and 30 minutes.
- **Excursions**: MFE and MAE come from the highs and lows in between, adjusted for direction, with the minutes to each.
- **Costs**: net return subtracts 12 bps round trip (brokerage, STT, exchange and stamp ≈ 5 bps, plus slippage ≈ 3.5 bps per side).

Outcomes are written to `<store>/945/<namespace>/outcomes/` after the decision. Live outcomes are
provisional from 10:16 and final after the close. The selector never sees its own day's outcome.

## Storage and immutability

`<store>/945/live/decisions/<date>.json` is created with `O_EXCL` and mode 0444. Writing a different
decision for the same day raises an error. Each decision carries:

- the input hash (all bars by 09:45 and the reference);
- the configuration hash;
- the training-set hash;
- the v2 coefficient hash;
- a decision hash over the content.

Historical replays use the namespace `replay-<selector version>`. Training records
(`<store>/945/training/<date>.v1.npz`) hold the 09:45 matrix and realised outcomes for every stock. They
are shared by live and replay and are only ever used for later dates.

## Live behaviour and latency

1. PSYGRID writes the 09:44 bar to the minute stream about 3 s after 09:45:00.
2. The intelligence runner polls every 2 s, steps the engine, then decides. In the sandbox the decision step
   takes about 1 s at 989 stocks, so the decision exists by about 09:45:07.
3. Day summaries, market-state profiles and the response model are cached outside market hours, so 09:45 does
   no history loading.

## Reconstruction and evaluation

`python 945.py replay` walks every qualified session in date order. For each one it:

1. freezes at 09:45 and decides;
2. stores the decision;
3. then measures the outcome and adds that day's training record.

The first five sessions only build history. `python 945.py report` summarises, per horizon:

- hit rate (Wilson interval);
- mean, median and standard deviation of returns;
- mean net return with its 95% interval and t;
- MFE and MAE;
- cumulative net return;
- splits by model and by market state.

The verdict is pre-registered:
- `SUPPORTED` if the mean net 15-minute return's 95% interval is above zero, with at least 30 decisions;
- `REJECTED` if the interval is below zero;
- `UNPROVEN` otherwise.
